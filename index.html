About

I am a Teaching Assistant Professor in the Department of Agricultural Economics and Agribusiness at the University of Arkansas. My research sits at the intersection of production economics and natural-resource and environmental economics.

My current work examines irrigation as climate adaptation, agricultural productivity and groundwater use, climate response in Brazilian crop systems, and the broader effects of energy policy.

Climate Response of Second-Season Corn in Brazil

Bruno Pinto and Marcelo José Braga

Using municipal yields, daily weather, and region-specific phenological calendars for 2003–2023, we estimate how extreme heat and water deficit affect Brazil’s second-season corn. One additional killing degree day above 29°C lowers yields by about 0.10%, and a uniform 1°C warming reduces predicted yields by 8.2%.

Irrigation Efficiency and Productivity in the Ogallala Aquifer Region: The Role of Weather

Bruno Pinto, Lilyan Fulginiti, and Richard Perrin

Using three-stage Data Envelopment Analysis and the Global Malmquist Index, we compare county-level irrigation efficiency and productivity measures with and without local weather controls. Accounting for weather raises average irrigation-oriented efficiency by 21.4%, doubles the share of counties on the best-practice frontier, and increases measured productivity growth from 120% to 146%.
